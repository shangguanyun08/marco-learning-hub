Question 1 Your role in Marco's education

What do you see as your main role in your child's education?

Supporting Marco as he works toward goals he sets for himself is our main role in his education. Marco holds himself to a high standard and is self-motivated, so we rarely need to push him. What he needs from us is patience, practical help, and someone in his corner when it gets hard.

Last school year, Marco prepared for his Level 5 Certificate of Merit exam in piano. He took online practice theory tests regularly, and I printed them for him to use in his preparation.

The performance recordings proved more challenging. His longest piece was about four minutes. Many times he played for three minutes, slipped in the last measure, and wanted to start over. Often I could not even hear the mistake, but he could. We helped with recording and kept the house quiet. When one piece became frustrating, we set it aside, recorded the shorter pieces first, and came back to it later with more confidence. When he got upset, I did not tell him to try harder. I reassured him: "It's okay. Mom will be right here. Whenever you feel ready, we'll start." He finished his recordings in February 2026 and earned excellent ratings in theory and performance.

We also try to encourage Marco's curiosity by exploring his interests together. On Saturdays, his dad and I read his Chinese school lessons with him. When his class read the story of Tian Ji's horse race, we talked about what was happening in China at that time. These conversations connect his language lessons with history and culture. His interest in the World Cup has also led to family conversations about the teams and countries taking part. At home, his dad talks with him about business and personal finance, which interests Marco.

For us, supporting his education means making time for these conversations, helping when he needs it, and giving him room to do the work himself. We hope he learns that he can work through challenges, take a break when he needs one, and ask for support without losing ownership of his goals.

Question 2 Why Sage Hill

Explain your interest in sending your child to Sage Hill.

We admire Sage Hill's encouragement of students to become independent and discover their interests. Marco is especially drawn to the humanities and creative work. He writes and illustrates comics, enjoys history, and designs projects with friends. We want him to develop these interests while continuing to explore a broad academic program. Sage Hill's middle school offerings in visual art, digital art, journalism, and debate connect directly with interests he already pursues on his own.

Much of what we know about Sage Hill comes from our colleagues. Darren Chee, whose child attends Sage Hill, was the first to tell us about the school. Later, Jingguang Wang, whose oldest is now in eleventh grade there, told us about their experience with Sage Hill in much greater detail. Several other colleagues we know well also have children there, including Guansheng Li's child in ninth grade and Jingguang's second child in eighth grade. Hearing about their families' real experiences gave us a clear picture of the school.

Marco attended summer camp at Sage Hill, and our family later attended an Open House. What impressed us most was how readily teachers approached us. At the middle school table, a staff member began telling us about the program and immediately recognized Marco from summer camp. At the History table, the middle school U.S. History teacher explained what seventh graders study. Teacher after teacher invited our questions and answered warmly. The students were also kind, confident, and quick to help visiting families. These encounters made the school feel welcoming and personal.

When we asked Marco what he liked about Sage Hill, his first answer was the cafeteria. We appreciated his honest, very practical answer; he already has his own reasons to feel comfortable there.

The Sage Hill brochure describes students who create, solve problems, work hard, laugh, and explore. When we read that, we recognized Marco. Sage Hill is the only private middle school we are applying to because we can picture him thriving there, with his curiosity encouraged and his creativity given room to grow. In our experience, middle and high school are formative years, when young people begin to shape their values, their understanding of the world, and their hopes for the future. We trust Sage Hill to help Marco explore, think for himself, and develop his character as he grows toward adulthood. We also hope he will contribute his humor, ideas, and care for others to the school community.

Question 4 Marco's academic and personal strengths and weaknesses

Describe your child's academic and personal strengths and weaknesses.

Marco is a creative, persistent, and funny boy who takes pride in his work. His high standards help him follow through; occasionally, they also lead him to put too much pressure on himself.

His persistence shows in taekwondo. I still remember a class when he was in first grade. He and another child of similar size had a rope tied around their waists and crawled in opposite directions, each trying to pull the other. Neither gave way for two or three minutes. The coach called a tie and had them try again. After another long effort, Marco finally won, and the parents watching cheered for both boys. That same determination has stayed with him. He went from white belt to black belt in two years and continued because he loved it. He has earned gold and silver medals, including a silver in 2025. For that event, he put together his own routine and competed without a coach beside him. He went in hoping to do his best and was overjoyed to come home with a medal.

In fourth grade, Marco entered APAAS, Irvine's program for advanced students, which meant changing schools and leaving his old classmates. He has managed the more challenging work while doing his homework and preparing for tests independently. Writing and history are particular interests. For a U.S. history project, he wrote and illustrated his own booklet, "The Wars of the American Revolution." Since he was five, he has also made his own books, writing the stories, drawing the illustrations, and binding the pages together.

In first and second grade, he wrote a comic series called Marshmallow and Onion and completed its ending in third grade. He later created "The Adventure of Coral the Axolotl" with a friend who drew the pictures. He has continued taking art lessons and has won two art contest awards. This fall, he brought a watercolor from summer art camp to his sixth-grade class introduction, and his teacher chose to display it in the classroom.

Recently, Marco and a close friend designed and 3D-printed turtles for classmates. Marco drew the designs, logo, and flyer, and the boys priced the turtles in their school's reward tickets, called "Gotchas." The school later stopped the sales. The project brought together several of his interests: making things, sharing ideas with a friend, and finding an audience for his designs.

His principal has remarked on how articulate he is, and Marco now competes in speech and debate in the middle school division.

He is a thoughtful friend, too. After a close friend mentioned that she liked frogs, he sewed a frog for her from fabric and cotton. We only learned later that it was a gift.

Humor is another part of Marco's personality. He is comfortable being playful in front of others. At an all-school assembly this fall, he volunteered for a jumping activity, then added a few moves of the "Ke Mu San" dance. The students burst out laughing, and his little brother Harry thought it was hilarious.

His main area for growth is handling mistakes without becoming too hard on himself. A small slip near the end of a piano piece can leave him frustrated and self-critical. We have learned that giving him space helps more than pushing or lecturing. He is getting better at pausing, setting a difficult project aside, and returning to it later. More often now, he simply says, "We need to redo it," and starts again. Frustration still occurs, but it passes faster. He is learning when to keep trying, when to take a break, and how to accept mistakes as part of learning.
